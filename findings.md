# EuroFoods S.A. — Findings & Management Summary

*FP&A analysis of 2 years of performance (Jan 2023 – Dec 2024). All figures in
EUR. Source: `case_study_eurofoods.xlsx`; methodology in `data_profile.md`.*

**Headline:** Revenue grew **+5.4% YoY** to **€2.36M** in 2024, but the group
landed **€226.5K (−4.7%) below budget** — and that miss is **almost entirely a
volume story, not a pricing one**. Margins are healthy and remarkably stable
(~38.7%) but planning assumptions were too optimistic across the board.

---

## Answers to the analytical questions

### 1. Which country dominates — revenue vs margin?
**France leads on revenue, but margin is effectively a three-way tie.**

| Country | Revenue (2 yrs) | Gross Margin | Margin % |
|---|---|---|---|
| **France** | **€1,817,440** | €703,323 | 38.7% |
| Germany | €1,542,888 | €598,824 | **38.8%** |
| UK | €1,244,091 | €481,436 | 38.7% |

France is the clear **revenue** engine (~39% of the group). On **profitability
rate** the three markets are statistically indistinguishable (38.7–38.8%), so
revenue scale — not margin — is what separates them. The most profitable single
**country × segment** cell by gross-margin € is **France × Hypermarket
(€246.6K)**; by margin **%** it is **Germany × Supermarket (38.9%)**.

### 2. Which segment is the most dynamic?
**Supermarket**, growing **+5.9% YoY** — the fastest of the four channels and
the largest by revenue (€793K in 2024).

| Segment | 2023 | 2024 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Supermarket** | €748,908 | €793,394 | **+5.9%** |
| Convenience | €364,461 | €384,065 | +5.4% |
| Hypermarket | €618,725 | €651,484 | +5.3% |
| Online | €509,282 | €534,098 | +4.9% |

Notably, **Online is the *slowest*-growing channel** here — counter to the usual
e-commerce narrative (see Q9).

### 3. Which products are below their category target margin?
**10 of 20 SKUs** sit below target — but **all by trivial margins** (gaps of
**−0.06 to −0.29 pp**). These are realised-vs-list price erosion, *not*
structurally unprofitable products.

| Product | Category | Margin % | Target | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen Pizza Margherita | Frozen Food | 34.7% | 35% | −0.29 |
| Premium Orange Juice 1L | Beverages | 41.8% | 42% | −0.23 |
| Whole Wheat Pasta 500g | Dry Goods | 47.8% | 48% | −0.21 |
| Iced Tea Peach 500ml | Beverages | 41.8% | 42% | −0.21 |
| Vanilla Ice Cream 500ml | Frozen Food | 34.8% | 35% | −0.17 |
| Avocados Pack of 3 | Fresh Produce | 29.9% | 30% | −0.14 |
| Dark Chocolate 200g | Dry Goods | 47.9% | 48% | −0.13 |
| Organic Apple Juice 750ml | Beverages | 41.9% | 42% | −0.10 |
| Mixed Vegetables 1kg | Frozen Food | 34.9% | 35% | −0.10 |
| Organic Granola 500g | Dry Goods | 47.9% | 48% | −0.06 |

**Discontinuation view:** no product is a clear "dog" — volumes are evenly
spread (every SKU sells ~56–60K units) and no SKU loses money. There is no
margin-and-volume case for discontinuation; the action is **micro price/promo
discipline**, not delisting.

### 4. Q4 2024 vs Q4 2023
**+5.4%** — €562,864 → **€593,499** (+€30,635). The holiday quarter grew in line
with the full-year trend; no acceleration or deceleration into year-end.

### 5. Total variance vs budget
**−€226,548 (−4.7%), unfavourable.** Actual €4,604,418 vs budget €4,830,965 over
the two years. **Every** country and **every** category came in under plan —
this is a systematic budgeting-optimism issue, not a localised problem.

### 6. Which country misses its budget the most?
Depends on the lens, but the gap is uniform:

| Country | Variance € | Variance % |
|---|---|---|
| France | **−€86,161** (largest €) | −4.5% |
| Germany | −€76,177 | −4.7% |
| **UK** | −€64,209 | **−4.9%** (worst %) |

**France** carries the biggest absolute shortfall (it has the biggest book);
**the UK** has the deepest percentage miss. By category, **Frozen Food** is the
largest absolute drag (−€69.2K).

### 7. Variance: price vs volume
**The miss is ~100% volume.** Decomposing the −€226.5K revenue variance:

- **Volume variance: −€226,001** (we sold fewer units than planned)
- **Price variance: −€547** (≈ €0 — realised prices essentially matched plan)

Pricing discipline held perfectly; the entire budget miss is **under-delivery on
volume**. This holds across all four categories. *Implication:* fixing the
shortfall is a **demand/distribution** problem, not a pricing one.

### 8. Which categories show the strongest seasonality?
**Beverages** is by far the most seasonal (coefficient of variation of monthly
revenue **17.2%**), followed by Frozen Food (13.1%), Fresh Produce (11.7%) and
the steady Dry Goods (10.4%). Group revenue **peaks in July** (€462K — summer
beverages/frozen) and **troughs in February** (€323K). Plan staffing,
inventory and working capital around the July peak and the Feb lull.

### 9. Online vs physical growth
Surprisingly, **physical channels grew *faster* than Online**:

| Channel | 2023 | 2024 | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| Physical (Hyper + Super + Convenience) | €1,732,094 | €1,828,943 | **+5.6%** |
| Online | €509,282 | €534,098 | +4.9% |

Online is ~23% of revenue but is **not** the growth leader in this dataset — a
reason the +20% Online scenario (Q10) is a *deliberate strategic bet*, not an
extrapolation of trend.

### 10. Scenario — Online +20% / Convenience −10%
**Net impact: +€68,413** (≈ **+2.9%** of segment revenue), on the 2024 base:

- Online: €534,098 × +20% = **+€106,820**
- Convenience: €384,065 × −10% = **−€38,407**
- **Net: +€68,413**

Shifting mix toward Online is **net accretive** because Online's larger base
outweighs the smaller Convenience book. The What-If page lets management dial
any per-segment assumption and read the net effect live.

### 11. Growth rate required to reach €2.5M in 2025
From the 2024 base of **€2,363,042**, hitting **€2.5M** in 2025 requires
**+5.80% annual growth**. That is **just above** the +5.4% actually delivered in
2024 — so the target is **achievable but not automatic**: the group is roughly
**0.4 pp short** of the run-rate and needs a modest acceleration (e.g. the Online
mix shift in Q10, which alone would more than close the gap).

---

## Top 3 recommendations

1. **Re-base the budget on realised volumes.** The plan missed by €226K and it
   was *entirely* volume — prices were on target. Reset 2025 volume assumptions
   to actuals and redirect the planning conversation to demand generation and
   distribution, not price.
2. **Lean into the Online mix shift.** Online is under-indexed on growth yet the
   +20%/−10% scenario is net-accretive (+€68K). Investing to grow Online
   single-handedly more than covers the +5.8% needed to reach the €2.5M 2025
   target.
3. **Run the calendar around seasonality.** Beverages/Frozen drive a pronounced
   July peak and February trough (group revenue swings €323K→€462K). Align
   inventory, promotions and staffing to the curve to recover lost volume in the
   shoulder months.
